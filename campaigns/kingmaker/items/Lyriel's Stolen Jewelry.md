# Lyriel's Stolen Jewelry

> **Rarity**: Unique (no known magical properties)
> **Status**: Lost — worn by [[The Wriggling Man]]

## Description

A piece of elven jewelry taken from [[Lyriel]] when she was abducted at the [[Nightmarch]] gate. [[The Wriggling Man]] wears it **openly, where it can be seen** — not concealed, not hidden to be discovered. A trophy.

[[Riven]] recognized it on him in [[Session 106 - The Thing He Wears|Session 106]].

> [!question] GM decision — what the piece actually is
> Danny established at the table only that it is *"a piece of her jewelry"* that Riven recognized. The
> specific object is still open. The strongest option is already seeded in Lyriel's own description:
>
> - **A hair pin** *(recommended)* — her introduction describes *"a loose braid pinned up with strands
>   escaped against her neck."* Riven has looked at that pin a hundred times. It's small, intimate,
>   unmistakable to exactly one person in the room, and its absence means someone **took her braid apart**
>   — which is a quietly awful thing to sit with.
> - **A ring** — the most legible "trophy" read; reads as claim and possession.
> - **An earring** — implies it was *torn*; pairs with a struggle at the gate.
> - **A bracelet or wrist-cuff** — she paints with *"sleeves drawn back from her wrists"*; ties the object
>   to her work rather than her person.
>
> Whatever you pick, keep it **non-magical**. Its power is entirely that Riven knows it.

## Properties / Mechanics

None. It is an ordinary, beautiful object. That is the point — see [[#Plot Relevance]].

**Fragile.** It is delicate elven work, and [[The Wriggling Man]]'s one real weakness is **area and splash damage 20**. The party's optimal answer to a worm that walks is exactly the damage most likely to destroy the jewelry along with him. This is the **AoE tax** in his revised encounter design — the choice between killing him *fast* and killing him *carefully*.

## History / Lore

Worn by [[Lyriel]] when she made the journey to [[Nightmarch]] of her own accord to answer [[Riven]]'s letters. She was taken at the city gate by a fey and trolls — the fey was [[The Wriggling Man]] in disguise, acting on [[Nyrissa]]'s orders. In the struggle she also dropped [[Riven's Letter to Lyriel|Riven's letter]], which is how the party learned she had come at all (see [[Lyriel's Wait]]).

He kept this. He did not have to.

## Current Location / Holder

[[The Wriggling Man]] — worn openly, Throne Room (K17), [[House at the Edge of Time]]. The fight for it is live as of [[Session 106 - The Thing He Wears|Session 106]].

## Plot Relevance

**It is the proof, and it is the taunt.** It tells [[Riven]] three things at once:

1. **She was real and he had his hands on her** — this is the fey from the gate, confirmed.
2. **She is not here** — every breadcrumb (*"the one with many faces keeps the elf-woman"*) pointed the party at this room expecting a rescue. The plan was right; the destination was wrong. She is held deeper in, **with [[Nyrissa]]** in M7c.
3. **He kept it because it was worth keeping** — he is not a jailer doing a job. He collects.

> [!tip] The mirror — the given and the taken
> Riven has carried [[Lyriels Amulet|Lyriel's amulet]] for the entire campaign: **the piece she gave him.**
> The Wriggling Man wears **the piece he took from her.** Same woman, two objects, and the distance
> between them is the distinction the whole House keeps teaching — what is *given* versus what is
> *taken*, love versus possession. Let someone at the table notice it out loud. Riven won't need it
> explained.

## GM Notes

> [!secret] GM Only
> - **Recovering it is the fight's consolation prize** — and it needs to be enough. The party came to this
>   room for a rescue and doesn't get one. What Riven carries out is this, plus the knowledge of where she
>   actually is. Give the recovery a beat; don't let it be a loot line.
> - **Two objects, one pocket.** Once he has both the amulet and this piece, Riven is carrying the whole
>   argument of his arc in one hand. If he ever gets to give this back to her, that's the scene.
> - **Optional, if you want the amulet to stay live:** near the stolen piece [[Lyriels Amulet|the amulet]]
>   goes *cold*, or pulls toward the throne and the plaza beyond it. Love still points at her — it just
>   points through the door now.
> - **Don't let it be destroyed by accident.** If a player nukes the room without knowing the jewelry is at
>   risk, warn them first. If they nuke it *knowing*, that's a real and interesting choice — let it burn,
>   and let Riven live with a party that chose the clean kill. Either way it should be a decision, never a
>   surprise.
