# Session 106 — The Thing He Wears

> **Date**: 2026-07-27
> **Status**: Complete — **ended mid-combat**
> **Previous Session**: [[Session 105 - The Thing That Cannot Be Forgiven]]

---

## What was established *(GM rulings — canon)*

**The party finally met [[The Wriggling Man]].** After a run-up seeded since
[[Session 100 - She Dropped Your Letter|Session 100]] — the abduction at the gate, Linzi's Sending, the
freed guards' whisper that *"the one with many faces keeps the elf-woman"* — the party reached the Throne
Room (K17) and the House's arch-deceiver is finally on-screen.

**[[Lyriel]] is not with him.** This is the session's structural ruling and it overturns a lot of prep. She
was never hidden in the throne room. She has been moved deeper into the House and is held **with
[[Nyrissa]] herself** in the moonlit plaza (**M7c**) — the Fable reached *through the throne he is standing
on*. He is the doorkeeper, not the jailer.

**He wears a piece of her jewelry as a trophy, and [[Riven]] saw it.** Not concealed, not a discovered
reveal — worn openly. It confirms he is the fey from the [[Nightmarch]] gate, it proves she was real and in
his hands, and it announces that she is *not in this room*. See [[Lyriel's Stolen Jewelry]]. *(The specific
piece is still an open GM decision — options are laid out in that note.)*

**The session ended mid-combat.** The fight is live; **107 resumes in initiative.** This is the third
session in a row to end mid-fight ([[Session 103 - The Shape of Where I Used to Be|103]] on the Rimebridge,
[[Session 104 - The First Mercy|104]] in the guardhouse) — worth noting as a pacing pattern, not
necessarily a problem.

## What actually happened *(actual play)*

> [!note] Shape of the night
> Two big fights with a short exploration bridge between them. The **Ankou fight carried over from 105**
> and ran long — most of the session — before the party got a rest, looted a telescope, and walked into a
> **fireball ambush** in the throne room that turned into the [[The Wriggling Man|Wriggling Man]] reveal.
> Third session in a row to end mid-combat.

### The Ankou, finished (K4)

- **Sorting the real from the copies.** Play resumed with the party still untangling which
  [[Ankou Assassins|Ankou]] were real: [[Pavel]] talked it through — *"the one on the other side of the
  door is duped and the one in our room is real"* — then corrected that the one in the doorway wasn't real
  either. He swapped [[Briar|Briar]] to **elven curve blade** form and moved into reach, with [[Riven]]
  pointing out that the curve blade *"gets more damage on subsequent hits."*
- **The doorway grind.** The restrained Ankou could still cast (no *manipulate* on the spell), escaped on
  **Acrobatics** after Astor corrected the skill, and answered with **Prismatic Spray** into [[Milo]],
  [[Lee]], and [[Lucas]] — catching its own duplicate through the door, which failed and ate the full
  damage. Riven worked **Point-Blank Stance** and **Archer's Aim**; Milo and [[Rindle (Construct)|Rindle]]
  burned most of a turn sorting out Lock On, Overdrive, and command actions before Rindle finally landed a
  hit and Milo threw his hammer through the doorway.
- **[[Astor]] versus the scenery.** Astor **shoved a shadow duplicate into a wall** on a critical success
  and killed it outright — then, later, **shoved another one into a chandelier**, killing it head-first.
  (*"Shouldn't be flying with those hazards hanging from the ceiling."*) The table worked out from the
  ability text that duplicates only persist while **sustained**, so unsustained copies simply winked out —
  after which the party **deliberately spread out to deny landing space** for new ones.
- **Riven narrows the room.** **Stifling Stillness** turned the chamber into difficult terrain and forced
  the Ankou to spend actions breathing; they coughed and took damage through the rest of the fight.
  **Hunted Shot** plus a hero-point reroll came up a natural 20.
- **Massacre kills its own caster — twice.** The party read the spell aloud and found the teeth: if
  **Massacre** fails to kill even one creature, the void energy **surges backward** through the line and
  into the caster. It happened twice. **Both times the caster died from its own spell**, each death
  spawning one more shadow double. One aborted Massacre was also rewound mid-cast once the table worked out
  the enemy's action economy didn't support it.
- **Cold iron, confirmed.** Lucas leaned on **Needle Darts with cold iron** between Battle Medicine and
  Treat Wounds rounds; the weakness the party learned in 105 paid off all night. A shadow duplicate clawed
  Lucas for **37** on the way down.
- **Lee ends it.** Lee tumbled through to the last enemy, worked feint/strike/trip, and finished it after
  it dropped to 1 HP. The final shadow double spiralled out of existence with nothing left to sustain it.
  **The Ankou are dead.**

### The bridge — bleed, a rest, and a telescope

- **Recovery.** The party sorted persistent bleeding from enfeebled; Riven's **Soothing Mist** cleared
  Milo's bleed while Riven kept his own, and Astor worked **Medicine** on him. Pavel crept the adjacent
  room for traps and called it safe.
- **The white marble room.** They rested 10+ minutes in the cleared hall — white marble, the carving of
  **the sun devoured by a great dragon**, twilight-forest paintings. Lucas ran **Risky Surgery** and
  **Treat Wounds** at master, and the table noted out loud that **Lucas's medicine has now surpassed
  Pavel's**.
- **Three doors.** Flooded tunnel, southern doorway, or the stair up. They checked the **flooded hallway**
  first — rubble and standing water, a rock pile to climb, shallowing out near the far door.
- **The Observatory (K7).** An adjoining room held a **large brass telescope** with an excellent field of
  view. Pavel searched it for traps and found none; Astor's Perception confirmed the strange part — it
  shows **real stars from the material plane**, with **no window anywhere in the room**. Valued at ~3,500
  gp, bulky but bag-of-holding-able. **They took it.** *(See [[Brass Telescope]].)*

### The Throne Room (K17)

- **The room.** Turquoise tiles, a false gallery / **upper walkway** with ornate wooden bridge sections
  overhead, a **spiral staircase**, statues, carpet, and the **marble throne**. Pavel read the ceiling
  murals aloud: **a wolf with a severed hand in its jaws; a great viper approaching a crib; a huge bear
  stalking a mother and child.** Astor's **Lore: Fey** said they aren't fey legend at all — they read as
  the **nature and mind-state of the realm itself.**
- **The ambush.** Astor scouted ahead and triggered initiative; a **fireball** erupted from the far side of
  the chamber. Pavel took the blame for the missed trap; Lee noted this is precisely why they let other
  people go first. Lucas answered immediately with a high-rank heal.
- **Finding the invisible.** Pavel used **See the Unseen** and found the **statues near the throne blurry,
  and the wall behind the throne blurry too** — *"an illusion and something invisible"* behind the throne.
  He used it a second time later to confirm: **the statues by the throne are the illusion**, and the enemy
  was standing behind them.
- **Reverse Gravity.** The hidden caster flipped the room. Characters fell **onto the ceiling and the
  undersides of the walkways** depending on where they stood, and the table spent real time re-mapping
  elevations. **Riven's winged sandals** gave him Gentle Landing; Astor's cape and acrobatics absorbed the
  rest; Pavel had his own fall negation. Milo got caught by the zone and was thrown to the ceiling with a
  small scream. Someone called it *"Wuxia territory."*
- **Cracking it open.** Lucas landed **Pernicious Poltergeist** with the frightened rider — the enemy
  **failed its Will save, frightened 2**. Riven followed with **Sure Strike** and a special arrow, then
  **Beacon Shot**, which burst the creature into **glimmering gemstone flecks and stripped its
  invisibility** (still hidden, but targetable). Astor **launched off the ceiling** to fall onto it: the
  landing missed, but the follow-up **grapple critically succeeded** off the frightened condition.
- **The reveal.** What Astor had hold of was **a humanoid mass of wriggling worms** — the
  [[The Wriggling Man|Wriggling Man]], on-screen at last. And the party saw the **jewelry** on it, and
  recognized it as [[Lyriel]]'s. The table's first read was the worst possible one, said out loud:
  *"Riven, I'm so sorry, that's not how I expected Lyriel to end up."* **They thought the worm creature
  was her.** *(See [[Lyriel's Stolen Jewelry]].)*
- **Where it stopped.** Milo commanded Rindle into Lock On and **Megaton Strike** (the table lost a while
  to how the extra dice apply); Lee moved into the open, perceived nothing useful, and effectively lost his
  turn to movement, though he shook the slowing effect on his end-of-turn Fortitude save. The round came
  back around and play stopped — **combat unresolved, half the party on the ceiling.**

  expected Lyriel to end up."* That horror lasted only as long as it took to resolve, but it happened at
  the table and it is worth knowing they went there — the jewelry did not read as a trophy at first, it
  read as **remains**. If the Wriggling Man is any kind of reader, that is the cruellest lever in the room.
- **The murals are about the realm, not about fey stories.** Astor's Lore: Fey came back saying the wolf,
  the viper, and the bear describe the **mind-state of the place**. Three images of a predator arriving at
  something defenceless — worth deciding whether they are Nyrissa's memories, her self-portrait, or a
  warning about what is coming.

## The endgame spine — **revised in 106**

> [!important] What changed
> The spine carried from [[Session 104 - The First Mercy|104]]/[[Session 105 - The Thing That Cannot Be Forgiven|105]]
> had **five** steps, with Lyriel's rescue as a separate mid-crawl climax that *taught* the party the mercy
> thesis in time to apply it to Nyrissa. With Lyriel moved into M7c, **steps 3–5 collapse into one
> confrontation.**

1. **The Gatehouse (104–105)** — the ashamed dead. *Some "monsters" want release, not death.* The verb
   **forgive** is learned and practiced. ✅ done
2. ~~**The courtyard Sard (105)** — the counter-example in miniature.~~ ❌ **never happened.** The party
   **skipped the courtyard** in 105 and entered the House by a north side door; the **Elite Sard was never
   fought** and is still alive. The counter-lesson arrived anyway, from a different direction: the
   **[[Ankou Assassins]]** in the Lower Hall (K4) are pure killers with no Shame and nothing to forgive,
   and the party fought them across the end of 105 and the start of 106. *Some enemies you just fight*
   landed — it just cost a real fight instead of a wasted Forgive Foe.
3. **[[The Wriggling Man]] (106 — in progress)** — the full counter-example, and now also **the door.** No
   Shame, no hostage, no honest reason to spare him: the purest statement of *some enemies you simply
   kill.* He guards the throne, which is the portal to M7c.
4. **M7c — the Queen and the healer, together (the finale).** [[Lyriel]] and [[Nyrissa]] in the same room.
   The rescue and the choice are now **one scene**, not two.

**The risk this creates, and the opportunity:** the old structure let Lyriel *say the thesis* — *"Some
hurts can't be cut out. You can only forgive them"* — safely, before the final choice, so the party could
carry it in. Now she says it **in front of Nyrissa, under threat, with Riven's hands full.** That is
higher-stakes and better theatre, but it is also easy to lose in the noise of a boss fight. **It needs
deliberate room.** Options for how to protect the beat are in [[Lyriel]]'s GM notes and
[[House at the Edge of Time]] (M7c).
