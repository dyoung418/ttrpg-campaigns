*** The Throne of Mirrors, Worms, and Falling Gravity
** Session Overview
The session opened in the middle of an extended, brutal fight against the Ankou Assassins in a cramped battle chamber with a doorway, adjacent room, and multiple duplicate enemies. The party spent a long stretch sorting out which enemies were real, which were summoned shadows, and how the duplicates behaved when their source caster was killed or failed to sustain them. The combat escalated through repeated spell exchanges, movement across the doorway, and a lot of rules clarifications about reactions, spellcasting, and how to handle the enemy’s summoned shadow doubles.

After the first battle finally ended, the group recovered with healing, Treat Wounds, Medicine, and Soothing Mist, then carefully explored onward. They found a safe-looking white marble room, a flooded tunnel, and a room containing a large brass telescope that appeared to show normal stars from the material plane despite the party being in a strange realm. The party claimed the telescope as loot and continued into an ornate throne room with a raised upper walkway, symbolically painted ceiling murals, statues, a marble throne, and a spiral staircase leading upward.

The throne room became the center of the session’s second major conflict. A hidden enemy launched a fireball ambush, then later revealed itself through an illusion near the throne. The fight shifted into vertical chaos when the enemy cast Reverse Gravity, sending the battle onto ceilings and undersides of walkways. The hidden caster was finally identified as a humanoid mass of worms, visibly tied to jewelry that appeared to belong to Lyriel. The fight was still unresolved when the session ended, just after the party regained footing and the table paused to discuss scheduling future sessions and Daggerheart play.
** Detailed Story Events
Battle chamber with doorway and adjacent room: The session began mid-combat against the Ankou, with the party still trying to keep track of which enemies were real and which were duplicates. Pavel reviewed the situation out loud, saying, “The one on the other side of the door is duped and the one in our room is real,” then clarified that the one in the doorway was also not real. He swapped Briar to the Elven Curve Blade form and moved into reach to strike the real Anku in the room. Riven pointed out that an Elven Curve Blade “gets more damage on subsequent hits,” and Pavel asked rules questions about Forceful and weapon damage dice while making his attacks.
Pavel attacked the real Anku, then made a second swing with MAP.
The enemy in the room was restrained, and the group clarified that restrained creatures can still cast spells if the spell does not include manipulate.
The Anku then attempted to escape, with Astor helping clarify that the enemy should have rolled Acrobatics rather than Athletics. After that correction, the enemy escaped and used its remaining actions to cast Prismatic Spray.
The table coordinated reactions, including Lee and Pavel potentially reacting to the spell, and Astor was able to take a reactive strike because they had rights to that token.
The spray targeted Milo, Lee, and Lucas, and Pavel manually applied the damage. The Anku on the other side of the door failed its save and took the full damage.
Riven then shifted into Point-Blank Stance and targeted the enemy in the room, using Archer’s Aim and a reroll with a hero point.
Another Anku drifted across the chamber, readied an action, and Lucas then used Treat Wounds / Battle Medicine on themself with a hero point reroll. The system glitched, requiring the table to manually calculate the healing.
Same battle chamber and doorway: The pressure around the doorway continued to grow as the party kept repositioning and targeting the duplicates. Pavel noted that one of the Anku was “hanging on by a thread,” and the enemy that had already used Massacre moved up but could not fit into the room because it was too crowded. It instead cast Ray of Enfeeblement at Riven, who took cover with lesser cover before the hit landed.
Astor then shoved a shadow duplicate into a wall, succeeding on a critical success shove and dealing enough damage to kill it.
After that kill, the table discussed what happened to the summoned shadow copy, with Pavel reading the ability text and concluding that the duplicate only remains if it is sustained. Since it had not been sustained, it vanished.
The party then spread out deliberately so there would be no landing space for additional shadow copies. Astor called for everyone to spread out, and Pavel agreed with the tactic, saying that walking underneath floating enemies would still be possible if anyone wanted to move into the room.
Milo’s turn centered on his construct. The table discussed whether Rindle should use Lock On, Overdrive, or some other action. Astor explained that Lock On was a Milo ability and that Milo often uses it to buff Rindle’s accuracy. The party spent time figuring out how many actions were needed for command, lock on, and movement.
Rindle eventually attacked through the doorway. The table compared the turret and projectile launcher options and their ranges, and Milo used a heroic reroll on the attack after a miss. He also threw his hammer through the doorway for his third action.

Same battle chamber: Riven narrowed the room with Stifling Stillness, explaining that it would make the area difficult terrain and force enemies to spend actions to breathe. He then attacked with Hunted Shot against the marked prey.
The attack sequence included a hero point reroll that came up as a natural 20, though the target survived the first shot and Riven followed with another strike.
The enemies began taking the environmental pressure from the spell, including coughing and taking damage. The table discussed that Stifling Stillness was the effect in play, not Enfeebled.
One enemy then attempted Massacre, but the table realized partway through that the action economy did not work out: the enemy had already spent an action coughing, then moved, and would also need to spend an action sustaining a shadow duplicate. Pavel corrected the sequence, revoked the Massacre, and returned the spent resources.
Instead, Lucas acted next and used Needle Darts with Cold Iron, then moved into healing mode with Battle Medicine / Treat Wounds. The healing routine took several attempts because of system issues and hero point rerolls, but it ultimately brought Lucas back up.
The party also discussed the enemy’s weakness, confirming cold iron and noting that the enemies were vulnerable in specific ways. Pavel observed that the remaining enemy had about 10 hit points left.

Same battle chamber: The party kept grinding down the remaining enemies and managing the duplicates. One Anku lost further ground and another enemy caster used Massacre successfully, causing the lethal void effect to rebound after the attack sequence.
The table carefully read the spell text aloud, realizing that if Massacre failed to kill even one creature, the void energy surged backward, dealing extra damage to the caster and everyone in the line.
The caster died from the rebound, and the spell created another shadow duplicate in the chamber. Pavel explicitly noted that both the physical table and the virtual table now had the duplicate present.
Astor shoved another shadow duplicate into a chandelier, killing it in a particularly absurd way: its head impacted the chandelier and it died. The table joked that it “shouldn’t be flying with those hazards hanging from the ceiling.”
The party again coordinated around weapon choices, with Pavel arguing with Briar about whether the weapon should be a chainsword or Elven Curve Blade. He swapped forms and attacked through the doorway with reach.
Riven added Tempest Storm and follow-up ranged attacks, then another enemy cast Massacre. The table rolled the saves carefully, and the rebound again dealt damage to the caster and to the rest of the line. This time the spell killed the caster and produced yet another shadow double.
Lucas then used Needle Darts again but missed, followed by another Battle Medicine / Treat Wounds healing attempt. He was visibly dividing attention between two directions while doing this, which was commented on at the table.
A shadow duplicate attacked Lucas, landing a claw strike for 37 damage. The party then noticed that the summon-like duplicate likely disappeared because its summoner had not sustained it.
Lee tumbled through to the final enemy, then attempted a strike and later a trip. The final enemy was eventually reduced to 1 hit point before Lee and others finished it.
Lee’s final movement and attack sequence ended the battle, and the last shadow duplicate spiraled out of existence as combat finally ended.

Battle chamber after combat: With the fight over, the party shifted into recovery mode. They discussed persistent bleeding, the distinction between Enfeebled and bleeding, and how to end the bleeding condition.
Riven cast Soothing Mist on Milo, which removed Milo’s persistent bleed, while Riven remained bleeding.
Astor attempted to use Medicine to stop Riven’s bleed, though the transcript does not confirm the exact end result beyond the attempted treatment.
The group decided to check the adjacent room for safety, then settle in and heal. Pavel carefully crept into the room while alert for traps and confirmed there was nothing attacking from the walls.
The room was described again as a white marble chamber with carvings of the sun being devoured by a great dragon and wall paintings resembling a twilight forest.
The party decided to rest for at least 10 minutes so Lucas could continue healing people.

White marble room with carved sun-and-dragon motifs: The party regrouped fully in the safe room and began significant healing. Lucas used Risky Surgery and Treat Wounds.
Riven targeted the wounded party members, and the group explicitly discussed that Lucas’s medic abilities had surpassed Pavel’s.
Pavel stated the room was the white marble floor room and checked that everyone was physically settled there before healing continued.
The first round of healing included Risky Surgery damage followed by healing, and then a second Treat Wounds attempt at master level.
The healing block also coincided with table chatter about the current time, the session schedule, and the fact that the group was now getting dark outside in the real world.

Flooded tunnel and adjoining room with telescope: After resting and healing, the party discussed three navigation options: the flooded tunnel, the southern doorway, or the stairwell leading upward. Riven listened at the southern door, then suggested checking the water passageway first.
The party inspected the flooded hallway, which included rubble and water and required stepping over a pile of rocks to continue. Pavel described that the end of the hall eventually became shallow again near the far door.
They opened an adjoining room and found a large brass telescope with an excellent field of view. Pavel carefully searched the room for traps and hidden features, finding nothing dangerous.
Astor questioned whether the telescope would show “real stars” and performed a perception check to inspect it. Pavel confirmed that it showed normal stars from the material plane, and that this was magical because there was no actual window there.
The party concluded the telescope was removable, worth about 3,500, and could fit into a bag of holding given its bulk and weight. They chose to take it as loot.

Ornate throne room with upper walkway and spiral staircase: The party then advanced into a larger throne room. The room had turquoise tiles, a false gallery / upper walkway, ornate wooden bridge sections overhead, a spiral staircase, a marble throne, statues, and a carpeted floor.
Pavel described the ceiling paintings in detail: a wolf with a severed hand in its jaws, a great viper approaching a crib, and a huge bear stalking a mother and child.
Astor used Lore: Fey to interpret the murals, but the answer suggested they were more connected to the nature of the realm and mind-state of the place than to actual Fey legend. The group debated possible meanings and remembered previous creatures and scenes from the campaign.
The throne room was fully revealed, including the upper-level walkway and the spiral staircase heading upward.

Throne room entrance and upper walkway: Astor scouted ahead, triggering initiative and then a sudden ambush. A fireball erupted from the far side of the chamber.
The party reacted to the fireball, with some members taking damage and others using hero points or healing responses.
Pavel flinched and complained about being blamed for missing traps, and Lee joked that this was why they let others go first.
Lucas then immediately reacted with a high-rank heal, trying to stabilize the group after the fireball damage.
Throne room under and around the upper walkway: The hidden enemy remained out of clear sight after the opening blast. The party realized the attacker was concealed behind an illusion near the throne.
Pavel used See the Unseen, scanning the throne room and noticing that the statues near the throne were blurry, as was the wall behind the throne.
He called out to the party that there was “an illusion and something invisible” behind the throne.
The hidden presence became marked as hidden, then concealed to the characters with magical sight. The party discussed that the square was known even if the token was not visible.
The group carefully confirmed the hidden enemy’s approximate position and prepared to engage.

Throne room, upper and lower levels: The hidden caster then unleashed Reverse Gravity, flipping the battlefield into a vertical mess.
The effect sent characters onto the ceiling or underside of the walkway depending on their position, and the table spent time working out the radius, height, and map handling.
Riven used Gentle Landing from his winged sandals to land safely. Astor’s cape and acrobatics let them reduce or nullify the fall, and Pavel also relied on fall-negating capabilities.
The table adjusted elevations on the map so that the room could be played on the ceiling and lower surfaces, and joked about entering “Wuxia territory.”
Lucas then used Pernicious Poltergeist with the frightened rider on the spell, and the enemy failed its Will save, becoming frightened 2.
Riven followed up with Sure Strike and a special arrow attack, though token visibility issues made the attack partly manual.
Astor then launched themself from the ceiling, trying to fall onto the worm-like caster and grapple it. The attack to land on the enemy missed, but the grapple attempt afterward succeeded critically because the target was frightened.
At that point the hidden enemy was fully revealed as a humanoid mass of wriggling worms. The table reacted with discomfort and surprise as Pavel described it plainly as a worm-shaped humanoid.
The party also noticed the creature wearing or attached to jewelry associated with Lyriel, leading to a reaction of “Riven, I'm so sorry, that's not how I expected Lyriel to end up.”

Throne room, reverse-gravity zone: The gravity chaos continued while the party tried to stabilize positioning and keep up pressure on the revealed worm creature.
Milo was caught by the anti-gravity region and ended up on the ceiling, producing a tiny scream as he was thrown upward.
Milo then used Overdrive successfully after a critical success reroll, and the table discovered that the Foundry scene had been hiding some of the upper-level sightlines because of the surface restriction.
Lee ran out into the open and tried to perceive what was happening, but the result did not give him anything useful. He then returned to the combat and attempted to strike the new enemy.
Pavel used See the Unseen again, specifically because the hidden enemy was still difficult to track through the illusion. He discovered that the statues by the throne were an illusion and the enemy was standing behind them.
Riven then aimed at the worm caster with Beacon Shot, and the shot caused the creature to emit glimmering gemstone flecks, stripping away its invisibility while leaving it hidden. This made the target more manageable for the rest of the party.
Astor later tried to leap from the ceiling across the gravity zone, taking falling damage on the way down and attempting to land on the worm creature. The landing attempt missed, but the follow-up grapple critically succeeded because the target was frightened.
The creature still was not restrained, and the table clarified what condition applied. This caused more discussion of what the spell and grapple interaction meant in practice.

Throne room, reverse-gravity zone: The worm-like caster continued to act, and the party kept dealing with gravity, visibility, and damage thresholds while the session wound down.
Milo commanded Rindle to lock on and attack with a Megaton Strike and hammer attack. The table spent a long time trying to determine how Megaton Strike and Megaton Strike CC worked in the sheet, including whether the extra weapon dice were being applied correctly.
Rindle eventually landed a hit and Milo later used a hero point reroll, but the damage math and extra dice application remained messy in the logs.
Lee moved and attempted his usual attack sequence, but by the end of his turn he had effectively only moved; the final attack sequence failed or did not produce the result he wanted.
The table confirmed that Lee was now out of the slowing effect, since he succeeded on the end-of-turn Fortitude save.
Finally, the round reached the top again. The party and GM paused mid-conflict to talk scheduling, live play dates, and a possible Daggerheart session. The transcript ended with the combat still unresolved in the throne room, the party upside down or on the ceiling in places, and everyone discussing when they could continue.
** Character Spotlights
* Pavel: Pavel spent the session doing a lot of tactical work and a lot of table-facing rules management. He identified real vs duplicated Anku, shifted Briar between weapon forms, kept track of hidden enemies, manually applied damage when the system glitched, and repeatedly asked questions about how abilities like Forceful, Massacre, and reverse gravity should work. He also had several memorable roleplay lines with Briar, including insisting, “She’s not the boss of me,” when switching her from an Elven Curve Blade back to a chainsword. Later, he also took a leadership role in identifying the illusion behind the throne and calling out the enemy’s location.

* Riven: Riven was one of the main tactical drivers of the session. He helped with weapon and damage rules early on, used Point-Blank Stance, Archer’s Aim, Stifling Stillness, Tempest Storm, Sure Strike, Beacon Shot, and several follow-up strikes, and repeatedly used his hero point to reroll attacks. He also handled a number of healing actions and spell-support decisions, including Soothing Mist on Milo and discussion of Lucas’s and Milo’s turn sequencing. His comments kept the party focused on action economy and positioning throughout the fight.

* Astor: Astor was especially impactful in both combat and scene control. They shoved a shadow duplicate into a chandelier and killed it, used Lore: Fey on the throne room murals, used the environment aggressively in the Reverse Gravity fight, and successfully grappled the worm-like caster after falling onto it. Astor also used Spellwatch to attempt to break free of the petrify-like effect, and later contributed to map and rules discussion with a lot of precise mechanical clarification.
Lucas: Lucas handled much of the session’s healing burden. He used Treat Wounds, Battle Medicine, Needle Darts, Pernicious Poltergeist, and later high-rank healing spells after the fireball and during recovery. He also tried to hinder the hidden caster with fear and support the party while the battlefield became vertical and chaotic. His role was especially important once the first fight ended and the group needed to restore health before exploring deeper.

* Milo: Milo’s turn management centered on Rindle, overdrive, and lock-on mechanics. The table spent a lot of time figuring out how Lock On, Overdrive, and Megaton Strike worked in practice, and Milo repeatedly directed Rindle to attack. Milo also got knocked around by Reverse Gravity, was thrown onto the ceiling, and later used his movement and command actions to keep Rindle active in the ongoing battle.

* Lee: Lee’s actions were focused on precision strikes, mobility, and finishing wounded targets. He used a feint, struck with finesse, tumbled through, tripped, and eventually finished off the last shadow duplicate in the first battle. In the throne room fight, Lee also attempted to perceive the hidden enemy, then later moved dramatically across the reverse-gravity zone to continue attacking. He was also the party member who was most obviously managing panache-style sequencing during the combat.

** NPCs and World Building
The Ankus: Duplicate-bearing enemies fought in the opening chamber. Their repeated use of shadow doubles, spellcasting, and positioning tricks defined the first part of the session.

Lyriel: Referenced through jewelry attached to the revealed worm creature. The table reacted strongly to this discovery, suggesting a connection between Lyriel and the hidden caster.

The hidden caster / worm-like caster: Eventually revealed to be a humanoid shape made of slithering worms. It used illusion, reverse gravity, and fear-like pressure to control the throne room fight.
White marble room: A safe-looking recovery room with carved sun-and-dragon motifs. The party used it for healing and rest.

Flooded tunnel: A partially flooded route with rubble and a shallower stretch near the far door. The party considered it a possible path forward.

Large brass telescope room: A room containing a removable telescope that showed normal stars from the material plane despite the absence of any actual window.

Ornate throne room: A grand chamber with an upper walkway, spiral staircase, statues, a marble throne, and symbolic murals of wolves, serpents, and bears in a sylvan style.

The realm itself: The discussion around the murals suggested the realm’s imagery was shaped by mind-state and curse effects rather than ordinary Fey lore.

** Plot Threads and Mysteries
Lyriel’s connection to the worm creature: The most obvious new mystery. The creature appeared to wear or have attached jewelry associated with Lyriel, suggesting a deeper tie.

The meaning of the throne room murals: The wolf, viper, and bear scenes were not fully explained, and the interpretation remained uncertain beyond broad thematic speculation.

The telescope’s magical nature: It showed normal stars from the material plane despite no visible window, implying a magical or extraplanar effect that could matter later.

The identity and goals of the hidden enemy: The creature behind the throne remained dangerous, invisible, and magically defended, even after being revealed as a worm-shaped humanoid.

The state of the summoned shadow logic: The table spent a lot of time clarifying whether shadow doubles required sustain, what happened when the source died, and whether all duplicates were independent or tied to the original caster.

** DM Notes and Observations
Pacing: The session was heavily combat-focused and very rules-dense. The first battle ran long, then the exploration pace picked up briefly before the second battle became even more mechanically complex due to illusion, invisibility, reverse gravity, and multiple movement layers.

** Player Engagement: Very high. The players were actively engaging with each other’s builds, correcting each other’s rules, and discussing action economy in detail. There was constant cross-talk about weapon choices, spell effects, and how to target hidden creatures.
Story Flow: The narrative moved cleanly from dungeon combat to healing and exploration, then into a larger set-piece throne room fight. The discovery of the worm creature and the Lyriel jewelry was the biggest story reveal of the session, and it landed well because the group had already spent time investigating the room and its murals.
